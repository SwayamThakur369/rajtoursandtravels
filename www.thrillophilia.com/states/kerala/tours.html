One of the biggest advantages of travelling in Kerala is its compact geography. Most of the tourist destinations are located within a four to five-hour drive of each other, allowing travellers to comfortably explore multiple locations in a single trip. When Should You Visit Kerala? (Season-by-Season Guide)

Kerala welcomes visitors throughout the year, but each season offers a different experience.

October to February: Best Weather and Peak Tourist Season

This is the most popular time to visit Kerala. Temperatures usually range between 22°C and 30°C, making it comfortable for sightseeing, houseboat cruises, wildlife safaris, and beach holidays. Most attractions remain fully accessible, and travel conditions are generally excellent.

The Kochi Muziris Biennale, Asia's largest contemporary art festival, is also held during this period in festival years.

If you plan to visit between 20 December and 5 January, book well in advance as this is Kerala's busiest travel period. Hotel rates can increase by 30–50%, and accommodations often sell out several months ahead.

One thing to note is that Eravikulam National Park remains closed from 1 February to 31 March for the breeding season of the Nilgiri Tahr.

March to May: Ideal for Hill Stations

Summer brings warmer temperatures, especially along the coast, where temperatures can reach 33°C to 38°C. However, popular hill stations such as Munnar and Wayanad remain much cooler, with temperatures generally between 20°C and 28°C.

This is a great time for travellers who want to explore Kerala's mountain regions. Wildlife sightings in places like Periyar Tiger Reserve can also improve during this season, as animals gather around water sources.

June to September: Monsoon Season

The monsoon transforms Kerala into a lush green landscape filled with flowing rivers and scenic waterfalls. Many travellers choose this season for its peaceful atmosphere and lower prices, with tour packages often costing 20–30% less than during winter.

Monsoon is also considered the best season for Ayurveda treatments and wellness retreats, as the humid weather is traditionally believed to enhance the effectiveness of oil-based therapies.

Travellers heading to hill regions should be aware that heavy rainfall can occasionally affect road conditions. Starting journeys early in the day is generally recommended to avoid both rain and traffic.

Where to Go: Kerala's 6 Travel Zones Explained

One of the reasons Kerala is so popular with travellers is its remarkable diversity. Within a relatively small area, visitors can experience scenic mountain landscapes, wildlife reserves, serene backwaters, tropical beaches, historic port cities, and vibrant cultural traditions. Most Kerala itineraries combine two to four regions, with road journeys between major destinations usually taking no more than four to five hours. This makes it possible to experience different sides of the state without spending long hours in transit.

Understanding Kerala's main travel regions will help you choose destinations that match your interests, travel style, and available time.

1. Hill Stations: Munnar, Wayanad, and Vagamon

Kerala's hill region lies along the Western Ghats, a UNESCO-recognised mountain range known for its rich biodiversity and cooler climate. These high-altitude landscapes provide a refreshing contrast to the state's tropical coastline and are often included at the beginning of a Kerala itinerary.

Munnar is Kerala's most visited hill station and is renowned for its tea estates, mountain views, and pleasant year-round weather. The region is also home to Eravikulam National Park, which protects the endangered Nilgiri Tahr, a species found only in the Western Ghats. Visitors should note that the park closes annually from 1 February to 31 March during the breeding season.

Wayanad offers a more nature-focused experience. Dense forests, waterfalls, trekking routes, wildlife habitats, and the ancient Edakkal Caves make it appealing to outdoor enthusiasts and travellers seeking fewer crowds.

Vagamon is one of Kerala's quieter hill destinations. Its open meadows, pine forests, and paragliding opportunities attract visitors looking for a peaceful alternative to the state's busier tourist centres.

2. Backwaters and Lakes: Alleppey and Kumarakom

Kerala's backwaters are one of the state's most famous attractions. This network of canals, rivers, lakes, and lagoons stretches across much of the coastline and plays an important role in local life, farming, and tourism.

Alleppey (Alappuzha) is widely regarded as the heart of Kerala's backwater tourism. Traditional houseboats cruise through palm-lined waterways, offering visitors a close look at village life and local culture. The city is also known for hosting the internationally recognised Nehru Trophy Boat Race, one of India's most famous rowing events.

Kumarakom, located on the shores of Vembanad Lake, provides a quieter and more resort-oriented experience. The Kumarakom Bird Sanctuary attracts numerous resident and migratory bird species, making it a popular destination for birdwatchers and nature lovers.

Traveller’s Note: Choose Alleppey if you want to enjoy a houseboat ride. If you prefer a calm and peaceful stay by the lake, Kumarakom is a great option.

3. Wildlife and Spice Country: Thekkady and Periyar

Located in Kerala's highlands, Thekkady is best known as the gateway to Periyar Tiger Reserve, one of South India's most important protected wildlife areas. Covering approximately 925 square kilometres, the reserve is home to elephants, Indian bison, deer, numerous bird species, and a range of other wildlife.

Boat safaris on Periyar Lake provide one of the best opportunities to observe animals in their natural habitat. Although tigers inhabit the reserve, sightings are rare due to the dense forest area. Wildlife viewing is generally most rewarding between February and April when animals gather around water sources.

The region is equally famous for its spice plantations. Guided tours allow visitors to learn how cardamom, pepper, cinnamon, and other spices are cultivated, harvested, and processed. These plantations offer valuable insight into one of Kerala's most important agricultural industries.

4. Coastal Kerala: Kovalam, Varkala, Marari, and Cherai

Kerala has over 550 kilometres of coastline, offering a mix of lively beach towns, relaxing seaside destinations, and quiet fishing villages.

Kovalam is one of Kerala's well-known beach destinations. Its crescent-shaped shoreline, Ayurveda wellness centres, surfing opportunities, and family-friendly infrastructure make it a popular choice for both domestic and international visitors.

Varkala stands apart as the only major cliff-backed beach destination in South India. Visitors can enjoy panoramic sea views from cliff-top cafés, yoga retreats, and walking paths overlooking the Arabian Sea.

For a peaceful beach experience, Marari Beach is a great choice. It has a calm atmosphere and is home to a traditional fishing village. Cherai Beach, near Kochi, is famous for the place where the backwaters meet the sea and for its historic Chinese fishing nets.

5. Kochi and Fort Kochi: Kerala's Cultural Gateway

Kochi is often the first stop for travellers visiting Kerala. It is a historic port city that has been influenced by traders from Europe, the Middle East, and Asia for centuries.

The Fort Kochi area is known for its mix of Portuguese, Dutch, and British architecture. Popular attractions include the Chinese fishing nets, Mattancherry Palace, and the Paradesi Synagogue, one of the oldest active synagogues in the Commonwealth.

Kochi is also a major centre for modern art. The Kochi Muziris Biennale, held every two years, is Asia's largest contemporary art festival and attracts visitors from around the world. Most travellers spend at least a full day exploring the city's history, culture, and attractions.

6. North Kerala: Kannur, Kasaragod, and Kozhikode

Often overlooked by first-time visitors, North Kerala offers a more authentic and less crowded travel experience. The region combines rich cultural traditions, historical landmarks, and coastal scenery while generally remaining more affordable than Kerala's southern tourist circuit.

Kannur is internationally recognised for Theyyam, a centuries-old ritual art form that combines dance, music, storytelling, and elaborate costumes. Unlike staged performances, Theyyam remains an active religious tradition performed in local temples and village shrines.

Kasaragod is home to Bekal Fort, the largest fort in Kerala. Overlooking the Arabian Sea, the fort is one of the state's most impressive historical landmarks and offers spectacular coastal views.

Kozhikode (Calicut) holds an important place in Indian history as the region where Vasco da Gama first arrived in 1498. Today, it is known for its rich culinary heritage, lively markets, and status as the cultural heart of the Malabar coast.

Which Region Is Right for You?

Each region of Kerala offers something unique. The hill stations are known for their cool climate and beautiful mountain views. The backwaters are famous for houseboat cruises, while wildlife reserves give visitors a chance to experience Kerala's rich natural beauty. The coastal areas offer relaxing beaches, Kochi is full of history and culture, and North Kerala is known for its lesser-known traditions and scenic landscapes.

For first-time visitors, exploring three or four of these regions is a great way to experience the best of what Kerala has to offer.

Best Things to Do on Your Kerala Trip

Kerala offers a mix of nature, wildlife, culture, and local experiences. Here are some of the most popular activities in Kerala that visitors enjoy during their trip.

1. Enjoy a Houseboat Cruise in Alleppey

A houseboat cruise is one of Kerala's most famous experiences. Sail through peaceful backwaters, pass villages and coconut groves, and enjoy meals prepared on board. Houseboats usually cruise for 4–5 hours before anchoring for the night.

2. Take a Boat Safari at Periyar Lake

Located in Thekkady, this boat safari takes you through the scenic Periyar Tiger Reserve, where you can enjoy beautiful forest views and spot wildlife such as elephants, deer, and a variety of birds along the lake's shores.

Note: Periyar Boat safari tickets cost between INR 225 and INR 1,500 per person, depending on the type of experience chosen.

3. Visit a Spice Plantation

Thekkady is known for its spice farms. Guided tours take you through plantations growing cardamom, pepper, cinnamon, and other spices while explaining how they are cultivated.

4. Walk Through Munnar's Tea Plantations

Munnar is famous for its vast tea plantations that stretch across the hills, offering beautiful views and a chance to learn about Kerala's tea-growing heritage. Visitors can enjoy scenic walks through the estates and visit the Tea Museum to understand the journey of tea from leaf to cup.

5. Explore Eravikulam National Park

This beautiful Eravikulam national park is home to the rare Nilgiri Tahr, a mountain goat found only in the Western Ghats. It also offers stunning views of the surrounding hills.

6. Watch a Kathakali Performance

Kathakali is Kerala's traditional dance-drama known for colourful costumes, detailed makeup, and expressive storytelling. Many cultural centres in Kochi and Thekkady host performances.

7. Experience a Kalaripayattu Show

Kalaripayattu is one of the world's oldest martial arts. These energetic performances feature acrobatics, traditional weapons, and impressive combat techniques.

8. Witness a Theyyam Ritual

Popular in North Kerala, Theyyam is a unique cultural and religious tradition featuring elaborate costumes, face painting, music, and ritual performances.

9. Try Bamboo Rafting in Periyar

For a more adventurous experience, join a bamboo rafting tour through the forests of Periyar Tiger Reserve. Guided by local experts, it offers a closer look at Kerala's wildlife and natural beauty.

10. Trek to Chembra Peak

One of Wayanad's most popular treks, Chembra Peak offers beautiful views of mountains, forests, and the famous heart-shaped lake along the trail. It is a great choice for nature lovers and adventure seekers.

What to Eat in Kerala: Dishes You Should Try

Kerala's cuisine is known for its fresh seafood, aromatic spices, rice-based dishes, and generous use of coconut. Trying local food is one of the best ways to experience the state's culture and regional traditions.

1. Sadya: A traditional vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf with rice, curries, pickles, and desserts. A full Sadya can include more than 20 dishes and is especially popular during the Onam festival. Expect to pay around INR 150–400 per person.

  • Where to Try: Bharath Hotel (Kayees Rahmathulla Hotel), Kochi; Mothers Veg Plaza, Thiruvananthapuram; local restaurants during the Onam season across Kerala.

2. Karimeen Pollichathu: One of Kerala's most famous dishes, this pearl spot fish is marinated with spices, wrapped in a banana leaf, and grilled. It is especially popular in Alleppey's backwater restaurants and on houseboats. A typical serving costs INR 300–600.

  • Where to Try: Thaff Delicacy, Kochi; Grand Pavilion, Kochi; houseboats and waterfront restaurants in Alleppey and Kumarakom.

3. Malabar Parotta and Beef Fry: A North Kerala favourite featuring flaky layered parotta served with spicy roasted beef. Widely available across the Malabar region, this hearty meal usually costs INR 120–250.

  • Where to Try: Paragon Restaurant, Kozhikode; Sagar Restaurant, Kozhikode; local eateries across Kannur and Malappuram.

4. Puttu and Kadala Curry: A popular breakfast made with steamed rice flour and coconut, served alongside black chickpea curry. Found in local cafés throughout Kerala, it typically costs INR 60–120 per serving.

  • Where to Try: Dhe Puttu, Kochi; Indian Coffee House outlets across Kerala; local breakfast cafés in Kochi, Kozhikode, and Thiruvananthapuram.

5. Appam and Vegetable Stew: Soft rice pancakes with crispy edges served with a mild coconut-based vegetable stew. A common breakfast and dinner option across Kerala, usually priced between INR 80 and INR 150 per meal.

  • Where to Try: Kashi Art Café, Kochi; Villa Maya, Thiruvananthapuram; traditional Syrian Christian restaurants in Kottayam and Kochi.

Kerala Essential Travel Tips:

Kerala is widely regarded as one of India's safest and most visitor-friendly destinations. Good tourism infrastructure, high literacy rates, and well-developed transport networks make it an easy place to explore. Keeping a few practical tips in mind will help ensure a smooth trip.

  • Choose Registered Houseboats: If you're booking a houseboat in Alleppey, look for a KIV (Kerala Inland Vessels) registration number displayed on the boat. This indicates compliance with safety requirements, including life jackets and licensed crew members.
  • Solo Travel is Common: Kerala is a popular destination for solo travellers, including women travelling alone. As with any destination, it is best to stay in well-reviewed accommodations and avoid isolated areas late at night.
  • Check Road Conditions During Monsoon: Heavy rains can occasionally affect roads in hill regions such as Wayanad, Munnar, and Idukki. Checking local travel updates before long drives during the monsoon season is recommended.
  • Follow Temple Dress Codes: Some temples have strict entry requirements. For example, Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram requires men to wear a dhoti and women to wear traditional attire such as a saree or long skirt.
  • Pay Attention to Beach Safety Flags: Always follow local safety warnings when visiting beaches. Avoid entering the water when red flags are displayed, particularly at beaches known for strong currents, such as Varkala, Kovalam, and Cherai.
  • Prepare for Humid Weather: Kerala's tropical climate can feel humid, especially for first-time visitors. Lightweight cotton clothing, sunscreen, and staying hydrated will help you stay comfortable throughout your trip.
