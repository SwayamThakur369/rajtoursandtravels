Best Time to Visit Rajasthan

Every season in Rajasthan offers a distinct experience. Raj Tours & Travels's seasonal insights are based on recurring traveller feedback across our 23,000 executed Rajasthan trips. Choosing the right travel window can make a significant difference to both your comfort and your Rajasthan tour package cost.

Peak Season (October to March)

Temperatures range between 10 degrees Celcius and 27 degrees Celcius, making this the most comfortable period for sightseeing across Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, and Jaisalmer. For those planning their Rajasthan tours for camel safaris, fort walks, and city tours without heat discomfort, this is the best time to visit. Cultural events like the Pushkar Camel Fair and the Jaisalmer Desert Festival fall within this window. Peak tourist inflow pushes hotel prices up by 20 to 40 per cent, and popular desert camps in Jaisalmer book out quickly. Early booking is essential.

Monsoon Season (July to September)

Moderate temperatures between 24 and 35 degrees Celcius, with occasional rainfall. Landscapes around Udaipur and Mount Abu turn noticeably greener, and the cities have fewer crowds. Hotel prices drop by 15 to 30 per cent, making this the ideal window for budget travellers. Desert activities in Jaisalmer may be limited, but the Rajasthan Monsoon Trip package is specifically designed to make the most of this season.

Summer (April to June)

Temperatures reach 32 to 45 degrees C, especially in desert regions like Jaisalmer and Bikaner. Wildlife sightings at Ranthambore National Park are at their peak during dry conditions, as animals gather around water sources. Off-season hotel discounts of 40 to 50 per cent are available. Daytime outdoor sightseeing is challenging; early morning and evening tours work best. Hill retreats like Mount Abu offer a cooler alternative within the same trip.

How to Reach Rajasthan

Rajasthan is easily accessible by air, rail, and road, with strong connectivity to major Indian cities. Jaipur, Udaipur, Jodhpur, and Jaisalmer are well linked through regular flights, trains, and highways, making travel across the state convenient for both domestic and international visitors. Here are some of the most common ways you can consider during your Rajasthan trip.

By Air

Rajasthan is served by 5 operational airports. Jaipur International Airport is the primary hub with domestic and limited international connectivity. Other airports at Udaipur (Maharana Pratap Airport) and Jodhpur connect major cities, including Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, and Hyderabad. International travellers generally fly via Indira Gandhi International Airport in Delhi (approximately 250 to 260 km from Jaipur) and continue by domestic flight or road.

By Rail

Rajasthan has over 4,600 km of railway track connecting it to all major Indian cities. Key junctions are Jaipur, Ajmer, Jodhpur, and Udaipur City Railway Station. Rajdhani and Shatabdi services link Rajasthan with Delhi, Mumbai, and other metro cities daily. Rail is the most cost-effective intercity option and is particularly useful for the Jaipur to Delhi or Jaipur to Jodhpur stretches.

By Road

Rajasthan has over 150,000 km of road network, including 20 national highways. NH-48 connects Jaipur, Udaipur, and Ajmer with Delhi and Mumbai. RSRTC and private buses, taxis, and self-drive options offer good connectivity from Gujarat, Delhi, and Madhya Pradesh. Private cabs remain the most practical option for multi-city Rajasthan itineraries, offering flexibility for early morning fort visits and late afternoon desert arrivals.

Popular Destinations to Visit on a Rajasthan Tour

Here are the best places to visit in Rajasthan:

Udaipur: The City of Lakes

Located within the Aravali ranges, the beautiful city of Udaipur is surrounded by seven lovely lakes and contains some of the most beautiful structures of Rajasthan. The City Palace by Lake Pichola is the centrepiece, with a complex that includes museums, courtyards, and stunning lake views. A boat ride at sunset is among the most memorable experiences in Rajasthan. One should not miss the Jagdish Temple, which is famous for its intricate carvings, and Saheliyon Ki Bari, which is famous for its marble pavilion and fountains. Travellers who spend at least 2 nights here consistently report a more relaxed and complete experience compared to those who rush through in a single day.

Jaipur: The Pink City

Jaipur opens most Rajasthan itineraries for good reason. The Amer Fort, situated 11 km from the city, is a UNESCO-listed hilltop fortress that combines Rajput and Mughal architectural styles. The Hawa Mahal with its 953 latticed windows, Jantar Mantar with the world's largest stone sundial, and the evening light show at Nahargarh Fort together give a rich overview of Jaipur's heritage. Shopping in Bapu Bazaar for juttis and block-printed textiles, or attending a traditional Rajasthani evening at Chokhi Dhani makes any Jaipur tour package worth 2 full days.

Jodhpur: The Blue City

Mehrangarh Fort, rising 400 feet above the city on a sheer rock face, is arguably the most dramatic fort in India. Built in 1459 by Rao Jodha, it houses well-preserved museums, royal palaces, and sweeping views of the iconic blue-painted houses below. Adventure travellers can zipline across the fort's battlements between 9 AM and 5 PM for INR 1,699 to INR 2,000 per person. The Clock Tower market area is ideal for local snacks like Pyaaz Kachori and Mirchi Vada before a rooftop dinner with fort views. Since Jodhpur sits conveniently between Jaipur and Jaisalmer, it remains a key highlight across many Rajasthan tours covering the state's most iconic heritage destinations.

Jaisalmer

The golden sandstone fort at Jaisalmer is one of the few living forts in the world, with over 3,000 residents still occupying its lanes. Patwon Ki Haveli with its 60 ornately carved balconies, and Nathmal Ji Ki Haveli showcase the craftsmanship of a different era. Desert camping at Sam Sand Dunes, with cultural performances and a Rajasthani dinner under the stars, remains one of the most sought-after experiences in Rajasthan. This is why we include desert camping in many Rajasthan itineraries, ensuring travellers do not miss one of the region's defining highlights. Many visitors are surprised by how memorable the experience turns out to be. As Tanvi Azmi, who travelled on the Golden Triangle Tour | Delhi Agra Jaipur, shared, "Camping under the stars with traditional music was the highlight of our entire journey." Staying 2 nights gives time for both the fort and the desert without rushing either.

Customer Insight: Raj Tours & Travels’s local guides also share fascinating insights about Jaisalmer Fort and other historical palaces, including how parts of the fort were constructed using interlocking sandstone techniques with minimal cement usage, an architectural adaptation to the desert region’s water scarcity.

Ranthambore

Home to one of India's most accessible Bengal tiger populations, Ranthambore National Park covers over 1,300 sq km with 10 safari zones. Jeep and canter safaris run twice daily (morning 6:30 to 10:00 AM, evening 2:30 to 6:00 PM) and cost between INR 1,400 and INR 3,500 per person for Indian nationals. The adjacent 10th-century Ranthambore Fort is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Safari zone allocations are random, and sightings depend on zone assignment; booking 30 to 45 days in advance is strongly recommended for October to March travel.

Mount Abu

Mount Abu is the only hill station in the state, and it offers a noticeably cooler alternative within the same Rajasthan tour itinerary that you’re choosing. Nakki Lake, the Dilwara Jain Temples with their intricate marble carvings, and trekking routes through the Aravalli hills make this a good 2-night addition for families and couples.

Pushkar

Centred around the sacred Pushkar Lake and its more than 50 ghats, this small town is one of the rare homes of a Brahma Temple in India. The evening aarti is a spiritual experience that draws travellers year-round. During the Pushkar Camel Fair, over 200,000 visitors gather for what is one of the world's largest cultural gatherings. Pushkar is best visited as a day trip from Jaipur or combined with a longer Rajasthan circuit.

Things to Do on a Rajasthan Trip

Here are the best things to do in Rajasthan:

Desert Camping in Jaisalmer

Swiss tents near Sam Sand Dunes with a Rajasthani dinner, folk dance, bonfire, and stargazing make desert camping one of the most requested experiences on our Rajasthan packages. Check-in is generally at 4 PM with check-out at 10 AM the following morning. Prices range from INR 1,000 to INR 10,000 per person, depending on camp category and inclusions. The Best of Rajasthan package with a free Jaisalmer camping experience, starting at INR 35,200, is our most popular option for travellers who want the complete desert experience included.

Wildlife Safari in Ranthambore National Park

Morning and evening jeep safaris across Ranthambore's 10 zones are the primary draw for wildlife enthusiasts. Morning slots (6:30 to 10:00 AM) generally offer better sighting conditions. Beyond tigers, the park supports leopards, sloth bears, crocodiles, and over 300 bird species. Entry for Indian nationals costs INR 1,400 to INR 3,500 per person by jeep or canter. Advance booking is non-negotiable during peak season.

Hot Air Ballooning in Jaipur

Aerial views of Amer Fort and the Aravalli hills from a hot air balloon are available during early morning (5:30 to 7:30 AM) and evening (4:30 to 6:30 PM) slots. Flights last approximately 60 minutes and are weather-dependent. Prices range from INR 13,500 to INR 50,000 per person, depending on the operator and package category. Many Rajasthan tours include hot air ballooning as an optional experience, particularly for travellers looking to combine heritage sightseeing with a unique aerial view of Jaipur's forts, palaces, and surrounding landscapes.

Camel Safari in Jaisalmer

Traditional camel rides through sand dunes with village interactions are best timed for sunrise (6:00 to 8:00 AM) or sunset. Rides generally last 1 to 2 hours with half-day options available. Prices range from INR 400 to INR 3,000 per person. Desert safaris are time-bound and weather-sensitive; camel rides for sunset views generally begin around 3:30 to 4:30 PM.

For many travellers, a camel safari is their first real introduction to the Thar Desert beyond the forts and cities. Since it remains one of Rajasthan's most sought-after desert experiences, we often incorporate it into our Jaisalmer itineraries to ensure travellers do not miss this iconic part of the region. As Binal Patel, who booked our Jaipur, Jodhpur & Jaisalmer | Journey Through Royal Rajasthan package, later told us, "The camel safari in Jaisalmer was the best part for the kids, Raj Tours & Travels made sure our transport was top-notch," highlighting how memorable the experience can be for travellers of all ages.

Ziplining at Mehrangarh Fort, Jodhpur

Multiple ziplines across the fort's battlements offer a unique aerial perspective of the blue city below. Sessions last 1.5 to 2 hours. Available daily from 9 AM to 5 PM. Prices range from INR 1,699 to INR 2,000 per person. Often included as an optional adventure activity in Rajasthan tours, this experience combines panoramic views of Jodhpur's blue-painted neighbourhoods with a chance to witness one of the state's most iconic forts from a completely different angle.

Shopping in Jaipur

Johari Bazaar is one of the best places to shop in Rajasthan for gemstones, gold, and silver jewellery. Bapu Bazaar is better for Bandhani fabrics, juttis, and block-printed textiles. Blue pottery is a Jaipur speciality worth looking for in dedicated craft stores. Best shopping hours are 11 AM to 8 PM. Budget INR 500 to INR 10,000 depending on what you're buying. Bargaining is standard in most non-fixed-price stores; always verify authenticity for gemstones and antiques.

Sunset Boating at Lake Pichola, Udaipur

Boat rides with views of the City Palace and the Lake Palace are available from multiple jetties. Municipal rides cost around INR 180 per adult for 20 minutes. City Palace jetty departures cost INR 400 to INR 600 for regular rides, while sunset cruises range from INR 700 to INR 850 per person. Best timing is 5:30 to 7:00 PM.

Key Attractions on a Rajasthan Trip

Here are some of the best attractions to visit in Rajasthan:

Amer Fort and Palace, Jaipur

Located 11 km from Jaipur, Amer Fort is a UNESCO-listed hilltop fortress known for its grand courtyards and the intricate Sheesh Mahal. Visitors reach the fort by jeep or elephant ride. The evening light and sound show brings its history to life. The Panna Meena Ka Kund stepwell is a 10-minute walk from the fort entrance.

Mehrangarh Fort, Jodhpur

Rising 400 feet over the city, Mehrangarh is one of Rajasthan's most impressive landmarks, built by Rao Jodha in 1459. The fort houses well-kept museums, royal palaces, and panoramic viewpoints. The zipline experience runs over the fort's rugged landscape for a unique aerial view. Inside, the view of the blue-painted houses spreading below is one of the defining images of Rajasthan.

Jaisalmer Fort

A living fort with over 3,000 residents, Jaisalmer Fort is best explored at sunrise or sunset when the golden sandstone glows against the desert landscape. Busy lanes inside are filled with temples, cafes, and local shops. Visit early in the morning for the best light and to beat the afternoon heat.

Kumbhalgarh Fort

Home to a 36 km wall, the second longest in the world, Kumbhalgarh Fort is the birthplace of Maharana Pratap. Set in the Aravalli hills, the fort offers panoramic views and an evening light and sound show. It is approximately 1.5 hours from Udaipur and works well as a day trip.

City Palace Udaipur

A must-visit spot in every Udaipur tour package, this grand palace complex sits directly on Lake Pichola and is famous for Mor Chowk, its three-dimensional mosaic peacocks crafted from 5,000 pieces of glass. The Mewar Light and Sound Show in Manek Chowk courtyard narrates 1,500 years of the Mewar dynasty's history every evening.

Ranthambore National Park

Covering over 1,300 sq km, Ranthambore is India's premier location for Bengal tiger sightings. The park has 10 safari zones accessible by jeep or canter. Wildlife sightings are most frequent during the summer months when animals concentrate around water sources. Morning safaris between 6:30 and 10:00 AM offer the highest sighting probability.

Lake Pichola, Udaipur

An artificial freshwater lake created in 1362 AD, Lake Pichola is the centrepiece of Udaipur. Boat rides cost approximately INR 180 per adult for a 20-minute municipal ride. Sunset cruises from the City Palace jetty range from INR 700 to INR 850 per person.

Jantar Mantar, Jaipur

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, Jantar Mantar features 19 astronomical instruments, including the world's largest stone sundial. Built in the 18th century, it is located close to Johari Bazaar in Jaipur's old city.

Food Experiences to Include on Your Rajasthan Trip

Having planned and operated thousands of Rajasthan tours over the years, our destination experts have seen that local food often becomes one of the most memorable parts of the journey. From royal recipes passed down through generations to street food that locals swear by, Rajasthan offers a diverse culinary experience for both vegetarian and non-vegetarian travellers.

Dal Baati Churma

No Rajasthan trip feels complete without trying Dal Baati Churma. This traditional meal consists of baked wheat dumplings served with lentils and sweetened churma. It is widely available across Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, and Jaisalmer and remains one of the state's most popular dishes.

Recommended Places

  • Chokhi Dhani, Jaipur
  • Natraj Dining Hall, Udaipur
  • Janta Sweet Home, Jodhpur

Laal Maas

Originally prepared in royal kitchens, Laal Maas is a spicy mutton curry known for its rich flavour and distinctive red colour. Jodhpur and Jaipur are among the best places to try an authentic version of this famous Rajasthani dish.

Recommended Places

  • Handi Restaurant, Jaipur
  • Spice Court, Jaipur
  • On The Rocks, Jodhpur

Pyaaz Kachori and Mirchi Vada

These popular snacks are local favourites, especially in Jaipur and Jodhpur. Freshly prepared and widely available in local markets, they make for a quick breakfast or evening snack while exploring the city.

Recommended Places

  • Rawat Mishtan Bhandar, Jaipur
  • Janta Sweet Home, Jodhpur
  • Shahi Samosa, Jodhpur

Ker Sangri

Made using desert berries and beans, Ker Sangri is one of Rajasthan's most unique regional dishes. It reflects the state's desert heritage and is commonly served in traditional Rajasthani thalis.

Recommended Places

  • Chokhi Dhani, Jaipur
  • Khamma Ghani Restaurant, Udaipur
  • Gypsy Restaurant, Jodhpur

Ghewar and Traditional Sweets

Travellers with a sweet tooth should not miss Ghewar, a honeycomb-shaped dessert closely associated with Rajasthan's festivals and celebrations. Mawa Kachori and Malpua are also worth trying during your trip.

Recommended Places

  • Laxmi Misthan Bhandar (LMB), Jaipur
  • JMB Sweet House, Jaipur
  • Jodhpur Sweets, Jodhpur

Safety Advisory for Rajasthan

These are practical notes compiled from our on-ground team's experience across thousands of Rajasthan trips.

• Desert heat and dehydration: Jaisalmer and Bikaner regularly reach 45 degrees C in summer with very dry air. Carry 2 to 3 litres of water per day and electrolyte supplements, as you may not feel thirsty in the dry heat.

• Temperature drop at desert camps: Between November and February, desert areas can drop by 15 to 20 degrees C after sunset. Carry a jacket or shawl even if the daytime feels warm.

• Road travel times: Jaipur to Jodhpur is around 330 km and generally takes 6 to 7 hours due to single-lane highways and village crossings. Start early to arrive in daylight.

• Avoid intercity road travel after 8:30 PM on stretches like Jaipur to Jodhpur and Jodhpur to Jaisalmer, where highway lighting is poor and fuel stops are limited.

• Fort visits require walking: Mehrangarh Fort and Amer Fort involve significant walking, especially during summer midday visits. Comfortable footwear and water are essential.

• Wildlife safari protocol: Ranthambore safaris operate in fixed zones. Tiger sightings are not guaranteed; follow guide instructions strictly and avoid noise or standing during sightings.

• Network and ATM availability: Desert camp areas and highways between cities may have patchy mobile coverage. Keep hotel contact numbers and some cash accessible at all times.

• Local market awareness: Shopping areas in Jaipur and Udaipur see heavy tourist footfall. Bargaining is expected, but verify authenticity carefully for gemstones, antiques, and handicrafts.

• Cultural sensitivity: Dress modestly in smaller towns like Pushkar and in rural areas. Covering shoulders and knees is recommended for temple visits throughout Rajasthan.

• Festival crowd management: During the Pushkar Camel Fair and the Jaisalmer Desert Festival, hotel prices surge 30 to 50 percent and accommodation fills quickly. Book at least 60 days in advance.

Which are the famous National Parks in Rajasthan?

1. Ranthambore National Park: Spanning over 1,300 square kilometres, Ranthambore National Park, is a renowned wildlife sanctuary that is best known for its tiger population. The park offers a unique opportunity to witness majestic Bengal tigers in their natural habitat. It is also the home to various other wildlife species, including leopards, sloth bears, hyenas, chithal, and 320 bird species. Rajasthan travel packages organise jeep and canter safaris which let you explore the park's rugged terrains and witness its incredible biodiversity. 

2. Keoladeo National Park: Keoladeo National Park, formerly known as Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary, is an avifauna sanctuary and a bird watching haven. Locally known as Ghana, the park is a mosaic of marshes, woodlands, dry grasslands and wetlands which provide habitats for diverse flora and fauna. Keoladeo is home to over 370 species of migratory and resident birds including the rare Siberian cranes and various species of reptiles, amphibians and fishes. You can walk, hire a cycle or go on a rickshaw ride to explore Keoladeo Park with Rajasthan tour packages.

3. Sariska Tiger Reserve: Nestled amidst the Aravalli Hills, Sariska Tiger Reserve is a wildlife sanctuary and tiger reserve covering grasslands, cliffs, mountains and dry deciduous forests. It is home to the majestic Royal Bengal Tigers, leopards, sambar deer, chithal, and numerous bird species like harbour quails and sand grouse. Visit the Neelkanth and Hanuman temples within the sanctuary, enjoy thrilling jeep safaris and nature walks, explore the diverse landscapes and spot wildlife in their natural habitat at Sariska Tiger Reserve with Rajasthan packages.

What are the best places to visit in Rajasthan with family?

Here is a list of some of the most cherishable places to visit with your family in your Rajasthan tourism packages:

1. Ranthambore National Park: Almost all comprehensive Rajasthan tour package for families include a trip to the Ranthambore National Park. This National Park has one of India’s largest tiger populations, along with other exotic animals like deer, peacocks, lions, etc.

2. Amber Palace: Amber Palace, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is an architectural gem that brings together the styles of Rajputs and Mughals. The palace is located on the top of the hill overlooking the Maota Lake and is surrounded by lush green gardens. To engage kids and teach them about the historical past of Rajasthan, Amber Palace hosts a light show every evening.

3. Hawa Mahal: Witness the architectural brilliance of Hawa Mahal and its unique pyramidal structure similar to the crown of Krishna with Rajasthan tour package . Thanks to the windows and jharokhas, the palace enjoys amazing ventilation and hence is named Hawa Mahal which means ‘Palace of the Winds’. Younger ones of the family are sure to have fun looking out of the windows and walking up the ramps to different floors of the Mahal.

4. Mehrangarh Fort: History buffs looking for attractions in Rajasthan travel packages that reveal the grandeur of Rajput architecture must visit Mehrangarh Fort. It is a 15th century fortress in Jodhpur that features seven huge gates, intricately carved panels, expansive courtyards, and ornate palaces. The fort also has a ziplining activity to engage kids as well as adults in the family.

What are the best places to shop in Rajasthan?

Rajasthan is one of India's best destinations for handicrafts, textiles, jewellery, and traditional souvenirs. Johari Bazaar and Bapu Bazaar in Jaipur are popular for gemstones, jewellery, Bandhani fabrics, block-printed textiles, and leather juttis. In Jodhpur, the Clock Tower Market is known for spices, handicrafts, and local snacks, while Jaisalmer's Sadar Bazaar is ideal for leather goods, mirror work, and desert handicrafts. Budget-conscious shoppers can find souvenirs for a few hundred rupees, while premium jewellery and handicrafts can cost significantly more.
