Spiti Valley — India's High-Altitude Cold Desert

Spiti Valley is a lesser explored place in the shadow of the Himalayas. It is an arid valley carved out by the Spiti River within the Trans-Himalaya region at altitudes exceeding 4,000 meters.

The terrain in this cold desert valley is desolate and dramatic, with barren valleys, eroded mountains, and minimal vegetation.

It is not only Spiti's rugged topography that is unique but also some unusual experiences you can have in this remote region.

Some unique experiences in Spiti Valley include the following:

  • Mail a postcard from Hikkim, the highest post office in the world.
  • Travel to Komic, one of the highest villages in Asia reachable by vehicle.
  • Explore the over 1,000-year-old Tabo Monastery, known as the "Ajanta of the Himalayas" because of its ancient murals.
  • See marine fossils in Langza above 4,400 meters that date back to when the region was covered in the Tethys Sea.
  • For animal lovers, Spiti is a place of extremely high population density of snow leopards.

Based on Raj Tours & Travels’s internal data, only less than 15,000 tourists visit Spiti Valley each year, compared to over 500,000 people who travel to Manali every year despite these unique experiences.

The low tourist influx contributes to the area's natural beauty and untouched terrain, without bustling markets and crowded tourist paths.

In addition to natural experiences, visitors to Spiti are immersed in local culture and traditions, particularly the Buddhist culture, simple and slow living, and traditional homestays.

Best Time to Visit Spiti Valley

According to Raj Tours & Travels’s booking insights, the best time to visit Spiti Valley is from mid-May through November, with variations depending on weather conditions, road access, and travel priorities.

Before taking the Spiti Valley tour package, you should note that the Manali–Kaza route remains closed from November to May due to heavy snowfall at Rohtang Pass and Kunzum Pass, while the Shimla–Kaza route stays open for most of the year but involves longer travel durations and slower road conditions.

1. Late May to June (Early Summer)

  • Roads from both Manali and Shimla sides typically open fully
  • Weather is pleasant, with temperatures ranging from 15–20°C during the day
  • Landscapes begin to turn green, with wildflowers starting to bloom
  • Fewer tourists compared to peak months

2. July to August (Monsoon)

  • The valley appears more vibrant with patches of greenery
  • However, landslide risks increase, especially on the Shimla–Kinnaur route
  • Temperatures remain comfortable between 15–25°C
  • The Spiti River flows at full capacity, creating dramatic scenery
  • Not ideal for first-time travellers due to road safety concerns

3. September to Mid-October (Early Autumn)

  • Widely considered the best time to visit Spiti Valley
  • Clear skies and excellent visibility for landscapes and stargazing
  • Stable weather with minimal chances of landslides
  • Temperatures range between 10–20°C, ideal for exploration
  • Harvest season adds cultural experiences in local villages
  • Recommended as the sweet spot for first-time visitors

4. November to April (Winter: Only via Shimla Route)

  • Manali–Kaza route remains completely closed due to snowfall
  • Access is only via Shimla through Kinnaur, with slow travel and possible weather disruptions
  • Extreme cold conditions, with temperatures dropping to -25°C in Kaza
  • Limited stays, transport, and basic facilities across the region
  • Suitable only for experienced travellers seeking snow landscapes and deep cultural immersion

How to Reach Spiti

The travel experience team at Raj Tours & Travels ensures that you get the most easiest and smooth travel experience hence, we’ve multiple options for people. No matter where you’re coming from, Raj Tours & Travels will make it easy for you.

1. Via Manali — Shorter but Seasonal

  • Route: Manali → Rohtang Pass → Gramphu → Chhatru → Batal → Kunzum Pass → Kaza (Spiti Valley)
  • Distance: Approx. 200 km
  • Time Taken: 8–10 hours
  • Best Time to Travel: June to October (as Rohtang and Kunzum Pass remain closed in winter due to heavy snowfall). Based on 2026 BRO (Border Roads Organisation) trends, the Manali–Kaza road (via Kunzum Pass) is expected to open in late May to early June 2026

If you’re crossing Rohtang Pass, you need a permit as per National Green Tribunal regulations to protect its ecosystem. Permits can be booked online (rohtangpermits.nic.in) and are released at 10 PM for the next day. Vehicle entry is restricted—only petrol vehicles (odd dates) and diesel vehicles (even dates), with a daily cap of 800 and 400 respectively, are allowed.

The cost is around INR 550 (petrol) and INR 700 (diesel). Without a permit, vehicles are turned back at the Marhi checkpoint, so booking 1–2 days in advance is recommended during peak season.

Private Taxi Cost: ₹4,000–₹6,000 per day depending on vehicle type and season

How to Reach Manali?

  • By Air: The nearest airport is Bhuntar Airport (Kullu), around 50 km from Manali
  • By Train: The closest railway station is Joginder Nagar Railway Station, around 165 km from Manali
  • By Road: Well connected to Delhi, Chandigarh, and Shimla via HRTC buses and private taxis

How to Travel from Manali to Spiti?

  • Hire a private taxi or rent a bike from Manali
  • Take an HRTC bus from Manali to Kaza (operates only in summer)
  • Opt for guided jeep safaris or shared cabs

2. Via Shimla–Kinnaur — Longer but Year-Round

Shimla–Kinnaur offers a gradual altitude increase for better acclimatization. While taking this route, Nako is an unmissable stop—visit Nako Lake and capture views of the surrounding snow-capped peaks.

  • Route: Shimla → Narkanda → Rampur → Reckong Peo → Kalpa → Nako → Tabo → Kaza (Spiti Valley)
  • Distance: Approx. 430–450 km
  • Time Taken: 2 days (with an overnight halt in Kalpa/Nako recommended)
  • Best Time to Travel: Accessible for most of the year (barring heavy landslides during peak monsoon)

The Shimla–Kinnaur route is preferred for its gradual altitude gain, which helps reduce the risk of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). Unlike the Manali route, this road remains largely open year-round, making it more reliable for planning. The journey transitions from lush green valleys to stark, high-altitude desert landscapes.

Private Taxi Cost: ₹3,500–₹5,500 per day depending on vehicle and itinerary

How to Reach Shimla?

  • By Air: Jubbarhatti Airport, around 20 km from Shimla
  • By Train: Kalka Railway Station, connected via the Kalka–Shimla toy train
  • By Road: Regular HRTC and private buses from Delhi and Chandigarh

How to Travel from Shimla to Spiti?

  • Take an HRTC bus from Shimla/Reckong Peo to Kaza
  • Hire a private taxi for a multi-day road trip
  • Opt for shared cabs or guided overland journeys through Kinnaur

3. From Delhi — All Options

  • Routes:

-Delhi → Manali → Kaza (via Rohtang & Kunzum Pass)

-Delhi → Shimla → Kinnaur → Kaza

  • Distance: Approx. 730–800 km (depending on route)
  • Time Taken: 2–3 days (including halts)
  • Best Time to Travel: May to October via Manali | Almost year-round via Shimla–Kinnaur

Delhi serves as the primary starting point for most Spiti journeys, offering multiple travel options based on time, budget, and comfort. Travellers can choose between the shorter but seasonal Manali route or the longer, more stable Shimla–Kinnaur route.

How to Travel from Delhi?

  • By Air: Fly to Bhuntar (for Manali route) or Shimla, then continue by road
  • By Train: Travel to Chandigarh or Kalka, then proceed via road
  • By Road: Overnight Volvo and HRTC buses available to Manali and Shimla; private taxis and self-drive options are also common

From Delhi, many travellers prefer breaking the journey with an overnight halt in Manali or Shimla before heading deeper into Spiti. Road trips from Delhi are especially popular among bike riders and small groups, offering flexibility to explore en route destinations.

Places to Visit on Spiti Valley Trip

1. Ancient Monasteries

  • Key Monastery: Founded c. 1000 AD. Key Monastry sits at 13,668 feet. With roughly 200–400 monks, this 1000-year-old monastery looks like a fort on a hill. It offers amazing views of the valley and holds many old Buddhist treasures. First-time visitors often miss the morning prayer session, which begins around 6:00a,. Arriving early lets you observe monks chanting in the main assembly hall before the day's activities begin. The monastery houses a rare collection of thangka paintings, ancient manuscripts, and clay figurines that predate many other Himalayan monasteries; ask a monk to show you the manuscript room, as it isn't part of the standard visitor circuit. Monks follow a structured schedule with prayers at dawn and again in the evening around 5:30 PM, so visiting at either of these times gives a far more immersive experience than a midday stop.

  • Tabo Monastery: It is one of the oldest continuously occupied Buddhist monasteries in India. It was founded in 996AD and is now known as the "Ajanta of the Himalayas.” This monastery has beautiful 1000-year-old wall paintings and is the oldest working Buddhist center in India.

  • Dhankar Monastery: Built between a cliff and the sky, this monastery-fort exists at a breathtaking spot where the Spiti and Pin rivers meet..

2. High-Altitude Villages

The upper Spiti region near Kaurik and Tashigang falls within a restricted zone due to its proximity to the China border. Foreign nationals require an Inner Line Permit (ILP) to travel to these areas.

The ILP can be obtained from the District Magistrate's office in Kaza; carry multiple photocopies along with your passport and visa as checkpoints are common.

  • Kibber Village: Once known as the world's highest village reachable by car, Kibber sits at 14,200 feet and is home to traditional mud-brick homes that look like they belong in a Tibetan painting. The village itself is a quiet, tightly-knit community where locals still practice age-old customs of farming barley and herding livestock through the short summer months.
  • Kibber Monastery: Separate from the village settlement, the monastery is a working religious site active in the daily lives of Kibber's residents. It is home to young monks from the surrounding area and holds regular prayer sessions. Raj Tours & Travels’s destination experts say that visiting in the early morning gives a glimpse into monastic life that most day-trippers miss entirely.
  • Komic: Asia's highest village with a post office, Komic is a place where people live much as their ancestors did, surrounded by snow-capped peaks. The sense of isolation here is profound. In winter, the village is almost entirely cut off from the rest of Spiti.
  • Langza: Famous for its ancient Buddha statue and the marine fossils found scattered across its fields. Remnants of the Tethys Sea that once covered this land millions of years ago. The statue stands with the striking Chau Chau Kang Nilda peak rising dramatically behind it, making for one of Spiti's most photographed views.
  • Hikkim: Home to one of the world's highest post offices at 14,435 feet, you can send postcards stamped from the rooftop of the world.

Don't be surprised to find a small queue of travellers waiting to send letters to every corner of the globe.

3. Natural Wonders

  • Chandratal Lake: Called the “Moon Lake” because of its crescent shape, this high-altitude lake changes color throughout the day and is surrounded by stark, barren mountains. It is accessible via a rough 8–10 km jeep track from Batal (motorable by SUV), after which a short walk leads to the lake. Camping is permitted in designated areas near Chandratal, with visitor numbers regulated.

Some of the key 2026 NGT and local regulations include:

  1. Campsite Restrictions: The camp should be situated away from the lakefront; only at authorized sites can tents be erected.
  2. Camping Season: Usually, campsites are open only during late June and early October.
  3. Vehicular Access: Vehicles should park at designated spots away from the lake.
  4. Permissions: An e-Aagman e-pass is necessary as the lake falls under an animal reserve.
  5. Waste Disposal: Camp managers must dispose of waste properly. Littering is strictly forbidden.

  • Pin Valley National Park: Home to rare snow leopards and other wildlife, this park features rugged, untamed landscapes with striking patches of green contrasting against the cold desert terrain.

  • Kunzum Pass: Drive through clouds at 15,060 feet, where colorful prayer flags flutter in the wind and panoramic views of the surrounding Himalayan peaks stretch for miles.

  • Dhankar Monastery & Dhankar Lake: Dhankar Monastery is accessible by road and offers dramatic views of the Spiti–Pin River confluence. From here, a 1.5–2 hour uphill hike leads to Dhankar Lake, a secluded blue-green water body hidden among rocky terrain, rewarding trekkers with pristine reflections and complete silence.

4. Offbeat Gems

  • Giu Mummy: See the mysteriously preserved 500-year-old body of a meditating monk. Strangely, people say his hair and nails still grow!
  • Mud: A village that looks like it was carved right out of the mountains. Life here hasn't changed much over time, and you can see traditional Spiti buildings.
  • Lhalung: Called the "Land of God," this village has an old monastery called Lahlung Monastery with a golden Buddha statue and masks with demon faces. In summer, the village is covered with wildflowers.
  • Demul: Stay with local families in this eco-friendly village known for its sustainable living and beautiful high-altitude hiking trails.
  • Kungri Monastery: The monastery's name translates to 'Snow Mountain' in Tibetan and it's famous for its calm ambience, perfect to explore on a Spiti holiday adventure.

Things to Do on a Spiti Valley Tour

A trip to Spiti Valley is never boring. Raj Tours & Travels’s destination experts suggest that travellers who indulge in adventure activities, cultural experiences and spiritual experiences see a side of Spiti that most miss.

1. Adventure Activities

  • Trek to Chandratal Lake: Walk along old shepherd paths to reach the beautiful "Moon Lake," where you can camp under a sky full of stars. Chandratal Lake is accessible by jeep track from Batal. However, the last 8–10 km is rough jeep track, not paved, but motorable in an SUV
  • Bike the Spiti Circuit: Ride a motorcycle or bicycle on the exciting roads from Manali to Kaza, crossing some of the world's highest mountain passes
  • River Rafting in Spiti River: Enjoy exciting water rapids between Pin Valley and Tabo, with tall canyon walls on both sides
  • Hike to Dhankar Lake: Take a 2-hour uphill walk from Dhankar Monastery to find a peaceful lake with amazing mountain views

2. Cultural Immersion

  • Stay in a Homestay: Live with local families in villages like Demul or Kibber, eating homemade food and learning how Spiti people live
  • Attend a Buddhist Ceremony: Watch monks chant prayers during morning or evening ceremonies at Key or Tabo Monastery
  • Learn Traditional Cooking: Cook with locals to make dishes like thukpa (noodle soup) and skyu (hand-shaped pasta) using local buckwheat
  • Visit Langza, Also Known as Spiti's Fossil Park: Look for 180-million-year-old sea creature fossils in Langza with local guides, showing that the Himalayas were once under the sea

3. Unique Experiences

  • Cross Kunzum Pass: Drive up to 15,060 feet for amazing mountain views and walk around the Buddhist shrine for good luck
  • Stargaze in Kibber: See incredibly clear night skies, perfect for taking photos of stars and spotting the Milky Way
  • Spot Snow Leopards: Join a winter trip with local guides to look for the rare and beautiful snow leopards
  • Post a Letter from Hikkim: Send a postcard from the world's highest post office at 14,400 feet as a special memory

4. Spiritual Journeys

  • Meditate in Ancient Caves: Find peace in the meditation caves near Tabo that monks have used for over 1,000 years
  • Walk the Kora Circuit: Join locals in their traditional clockwise walk around monasteries, spinning prayer wheels as you go
  • Learn Thangka Painting: Take a class with local artists to learn how to make colorful Buddhist paintings

5. Local Flavors

  • Sample Seabuckthorn Products: Try jams, juices, teas, and oils made from seabuckthorn berries (Hippophae rhamnoides), locally known as Sirmoli or Tsermang.
  • This nutrient-rich orange berry thrives in Spiti’s harsh climate and is a key part of the region’s cottage industry. You can buy authentic products from local cooperatives in Kaza and Langza.
  • Try Chang and Arak: Taste traditional barley beer (chang) and local spirits (arak) with villagers during celebrations
  • Eat at the World's Highest Restaurant: Have a meal at The Himalayan Café in Komic village, enjoying dumplings and noodle soup at over 15,000 feet
  • Join a Community Harvest: Help local farmers collect peas, barley or apples (depending on season) and join in the celebration afterward

Best Time by Festival - Spiti Valley Festival Calendar

Spring Festivals (March–May)

1. Dechhang Festival (March–April)

  • Significance: Spring agricultural festival marking the beginning of the farming season
  • Activities: Rituals for good harvest, folk music, sowing ceremonies
  • Duration: 1 day
  • Key Locations: Villages of Demul, Lhalung, and Dhankar
  • Special Customs: Seeds are blessed by monks before sowing; villagers perform a communal plowing ritual
  • Photography Access Note: This is a village-level community festival. Seek permission from the village headman before photographing ritual ceremonies - unsolicited photography during the sowing rituals is considered disrespectful

2. Saka Dawa (May–June; 2026 date: May 31)

  • Significance: Celebrates Buddha's birth, enlightenment, and parinirvana — considered the holiest month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar
  • Activities: Monastery prayers, butter lamp offerings, acts of compassion and charity
  • Duration: Month-long observations with main celebration on the full moon day
  • Key Locations: All monasteries across Spiti Valley
  • Special Customs: Many locals observe vegetarianism during this period; pilgrimages to sacred sites are common
  • Photography Access Note: Photography inside monastery prayer halls during Saka Dawa is heavily restricted. Contact individual monasteries at least 2–3 weeks in advance to request formal permission; some monasteries permit a designated photography window outside active prayer hours

Summer Festivals (June–August)

1. Ladarcha Fair (July)

  • Significance: Ancient trading festival with historical importance as a commerce point between Tibet, Ladakh, and Spiti
  • Activities: Local market with traditional goods, cultural performances, archery and other competitions
  • Duration: 3 days
  • Key Locations: Kaza (originally held in Kibber)
  • Special Customs: Traditional barter system is still practiced alongside modern commerce; displays of horsemanship

2. Chaam Dance Festival (July–August; exact dates vary by monastery)

  • Significance: Religious masked dances depicting Buddhist stories and the ritual defeat of evil
  • Activities: Elaborate masked performances by monks, ritual destruction of evil effigies
  • Duration: 2 days
  • Key Locations: Key Monastery, Tabo Monastery
  • Special Customs: Masks and costumes represent various deities and demons; sacred instruments including long horns and drums accompany performances
  • Photography Access Note: Chaam is one of the most photographed events in Spiti, but access to the inner courtyard requires advance coordination with the respective monastery. For Key Monastery, reach out at least 4 weeks ahead; unrestricted photography positions are limited and fill early. Tabo Monastery has a separate approval process through the head lama's office

Autumn Festivals (September–November)

1. Namkhan Mane Rinchen Festival (September)

  • Significance: Marks the harvesting season with thanksgiving rituals
  • Activities: Offerings to deities, community feasting, thanksgiving ceremonies
  • Duration: 2–3 days
  • Key Locations: Villages of Pin Valley and Tabo
  • Special Customs: The first harvest is offered to monasteries; community members share the harvest bounty

2. Phachuak Festival (October–November)

  • Significance: End-of-harvest celebration and communal preparation for winter
  • Activities: Prayer ceremonies, masked dances, community gathering
  • Duration: 1–2 days
  • Key Locations: Kye Monastery and surrounding villages
  • Special Customs: Offerings made for protection during the harsh winter months; special ritual to secure blessings for the stored harvest
  • Photography Access Note: Kye Monastery requests that visitors coordinate photography of masked dance rituals directly with the monastery caretaker. Drop-in visitors may be turned away from inner ceremony areas during this festival

Winter Festivals (December–February)

1. Losar Festival (18th- 20th February 2026)

  • Significance: Tibetan New Year, one of the most spiritually and culturally significant festivals in the Buddhist calendar
  • Activities: Monastery prayers, Chaam masked dances, burning of effigies representing evil spirits, family reunions
  • Duration: Main celebrations over 3 days (February 18–20, 2026), with the full 15-day auspicious period extending to March 4, 2026
  • Key Locations: Key Monastery, Tabo Monastery, Dhankar Monastery
  • Special Customs: Homes are thoroughly cleaned and decorated with auspicious symbols; special dishes like Khapse (fried cookies) and chang (local barley beer) are prepared
  • Photography Access Note: The first two days of Losar are deeply family-oriented and largely private. To photograph the Chaam dances at Key or Tabo on Day 2 or 3, contact the monastery administration a minimum of one month in advance. Some monasteries designate a fixed area for photographers; entering the ritual space without permission is not permitted

2. Gochi Festival (January–February)

  • Significance: Winter solstice celebration marking the passage through the darkest period of the year
  • Activities: Torchlight processions, local folk dance performances, communal feasts
  • Duration: 2 days
  • Key Locations: Villages across Spiti including Kaza, Kibber, and Langza
  • Special Customs: Elders bestow blessings on younger generations; ritual offerings to ward off evil spirits for the coming year

Spiti valley Tour faqs

What should I wear in Lahaul-Spiti?

Layer up regardless of season. Any well-planned spiti valley tour package recommends thermal innerwear, fleece mid-layers, a windproof jacket, UV-protection sunglasses, and sturdy trekking shoes — even in July temperatures drop sharply at night.

Does Lahaul-Spiti allow car travel?

Yes, a sturdy 4x4 or SUV is ideal. The roads are motorable May through October. Most spiti valley packages include self-drive or chauffeured vehicle options along the Manali–Kaza and Shimla–Kaza corridors.

How do you get to Lahaul-Spiti from Delhi?

Take an overnight bus or drive to Shimla or Manali, then continue overland into Spiti. Most travellers book a spiti valley tour package from Delhi that includes transport, stays, and a guided spiti tour itinerary.

How to reach Spiti Valley from Shimla?

The Shimla–Spiti route via Kinnaur (NH-5) is the most scenic entry point in any Spiti valley tour package. Drive through Narkanda, Rampur, and Kaza — roughly 412 km, taking 12–14 hours.

Which are the best places to stay in Spiti Valley?

Most Spiti valley packages include homestays in Kaza, Mud, Kibber, and Langza. Tabo and Nako offer monastery guesthouses. For comfort, Kaza has the widest range of hotels and guesthouses.

How do I get to Spiti Valley from Manali?

Drive via Rohtang Pass and Kunzum Pass — roughly 200 km taking 8–10 hours. The route is open June to October. This dramatic road is a highlight of any lahaul-spiti tour from Manali.

What are the famous monasteries in Lahaul-Spiti?

Key, Tabo, Dhankar, Kungri, and Lhalung are must-visits on any spiti tour itinerary. Key is the largest; Tabo, over 1000 years old, is often called the Ajanta of the Himalayas.

What are the most romantic things to do in Spiti Valley?

Stargaze at Chandratal, share sunrise at Hikkim, walk through Langza's fossil fields, or simply sit by a glacial stream. A custom Spiti valley tour package for couples can weave all of these into one itinerary.

Can kids go to Lahaul-Spiti?

Yes, with the right planning. The Spiti valley trip cost is worthwhile for families.The children above 8 generally adapt well if acclimatization is gradual. Avoid rushing altitude gains and choose homestays over camping for young travellers.

Which is better, Leh or Spiti?

Both are spectacular but different. Leh offers more infrastructure and connectivity; a spiti tour delivers raw, off-grid wilderness with fewer crowds. Choose Spiti for authentic village culture and solitude, Leh for easier accessibility.

How to prepare myself for Spiti Valley for the first time?

Before booking a Spiti valley trip, acclimatize 1–2 days in Shimla or Manali. Carry altitude sickness medication, warm layers, cash, and a basic first-aid kit. Download offline maps — connectivity is minimal.

How many days are enough for Lahaul-Spiti?

A complete spiti valley travel package typically spans 8–12 days. Seven days covers the essentials (Kaza, Key, Tabo, Chandratal); 10–12 days allows Pin Valley, Kibber, Langza, and a comfortable Manali exit.
